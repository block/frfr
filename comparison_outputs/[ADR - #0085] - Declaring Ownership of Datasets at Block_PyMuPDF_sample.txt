Context 
 
Data is collected as a part of business1.  Sometimes, the data Block relies on is inaccurate 
or missing. Downstream systems are dependent on data availability / accuracy. When 
accurate data is unavailable, it results in data outages, or even SEVs. 
1 Among other things, data is used to make critical business and product decisions, assess risk, 
and is also extensively utilized in our credit underwriting work.

Decision 
Datasets must have ownership declared at a channel level at every point2 after 
collection/generation. Ownership must be the smallest set of people taking responsibility 
for that data. Data platforms are obligated to support and collaboratively propagate 
ownership3 metadata in all cases. 
 
The following sections will expand on the concepts. At its core the expectations are: 
●​ From the point of collection and/or generation, data has an attributable owning 
group 
●​ The size of the owning group is reasonable4 
●​ Assumption/transfer5 of ownership is done with the explicit knowledge of the 
previous owner​
 
5 In this sense, “transfer” refers to data earlier in a pipeline having a different owner than later in a 
pipeline. The change in team responsibilities should be obvious, and not require mutual 
participation. 
4 “Reasonable” is subjective, but ideally in the range of 3 <= x <= 20 
3 To understand how ownership will work in practice, see Expectations of Dataset Owners. 
2 Data ownership points here are tied to the movement of data, with an emphasis on when data 
moves from one medium to another, where ownership attribution is frequently dropped. There are 
clear times when ownership might change in the same medium, so there must be a way to record 
these virtual boundaries.
